# Why Action Safety is the Winning Hackathon Strategy: A Feasibility Study for Building Credible, Governed Autonomous Agents

This report provides a comprehensive deep-dive evaluation of three shortlisted competition problems—PS02 (governance over integration), PS03 (action safety), and PS04 (traceability & lineage)—to determine which offers the most strategic advantage for a solo developer. The objective is to identify the problem that enables the most credible demonstration of governed, production-ready autonomous agent capabilities, with measurable safety, realistic architecture, and a clear path to enterprise deployment. The analysis prioritizes "Governed, production-ready execution" (weighted at 30%) and "Safety, deterministic controls & failure handling" (weighted at 20%). It treats "beyond-the-wrapper" innovation as a critical differentiator but subordinates it to foundational reliability and security . The attached document, "From Hallucination to Verifiable Action," serves as the initial framework, but its conclusions are rigorously challenged and validated against current market realities, technical documentation, and regulatory landscapes as of September 2026 . The feasibility assessment assumes a strict hackathon context where no pre-existing enterprise infrastructure exists; all domain-specific components must be built using only Lyzr platform access, open-source tools, and synthetic environments . A crucial distinction is maintained between a hackathon implementation and a real-world production architecture, ensuring that policies, agent graphs, safety mechanisms, and audit models remain production-oriented even when underlying data sources are simulated.

## Problem Statement 02: Governance Over Integration

The evaluation of Problem Statement 02, "Governance over Integration," centers on the challenge of creating an autonomous negotiation system between two distinct entities, such as a buyer and a supplier, while respecting their private policies and converging on an enforceable outcome . This problem statement directly addresses the need for governance in complex, multi-agent workflows where autonomy must be carefully bounded. The attached analysis correctly frames the innovation not as a simple dialogue between two LLMs, but as a system of "bounded autonomous negotiation with private policy state, convergence and enforceable constraints" . This framing aligns perfectly with the competitive landscape and the architectural principles required for enterprise-grade agentic systems. The feasibility for a solo developer is high, as the problem explicitly positions deep ERP integration as an enterprise-readiness feature rather than a mandatory prerequisite for a successful entry . This allows the developer to focus on building the core logic of the agents and their governing framework using synthetic data and mock interactions, mirroring a production architecture in spirit if not in physical connection .

The competitive landscape for agentic procurement is mature, dominated by specialized vendors like Pactum, which has successfully deployed AI agents at scale for major enterprises including Honeywell and SUEZ [[15](https://pactum.com/blog/how-enterprise-procurement-leaders-use-ai), [118](https://procurementmag.com/news/ai-in-procurement-the-industry-leading-vendors)]. These platforms demonstrate that the concept of integrating agentic workflows into existing enterprise systems like Coupa and SAP Ariba is not only viable but commercially successful [[17](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/pactums-ai-agent-surpasses-1-million-requisition-checks-performing-reviews), [18](https://pactum.com/blog/1-million-requisition-checks)]. Pactum's Requisition Alignment AI Agent, for instance, automates policy compliance reviews, reducing review times by over 4,000 times and processing over one million requisition checks since March 2026 [[17](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/pactums-ai-agent-surpasses-1-million-requisition-checks-performing-reviews), [18](https://pactum.com/blog/1-million-requisition-checks)]. Their success validates the core premise of PS02: that enterprises value autonomy provided within clearly defined commercial and governance guardrails [[15](https://pactum.com/blog/how-enterprise-procurement-leaders-use-ai)]. For a hackathon participant, this means the problem is grounded in a proven business use case, providing a solid foundation upon which to build a demonstrably valuable solution. The key takeaway is that innovation lies in the application of agentic principles to specific, high-value processes like procurement, rather than inventing new processes from scratch [[15](https://pactum.com/blog/how-enterprise-procurement-leaders-use-ai)]. A solo developer can therefore focus on implementing a robust version of this established pattern within the constrained hackathon environment.

For a solo developer, the implementation of PS02 is highly manageable under the stated feasibility rules . The assumption of zero pre-existing infrastructure is a significant advantage, forcing a clean-sheet design that focuses purely on the agent architecture and governance layer . The development effort can be broken down into several manageable components:
*   **Synthetic Data Generation:** Creating synthetic suppliers, products, and buyer policies is straightforward and essential for establishing the negotiation context .
*   **Agent Design:** Designing two distinct agent personas (e.g., Buyer Agent, Supplier Agent) with specific roles and objectives. Each agent would operate with its own "private policy envelope," a concept that translates directly to Lyzr's granular access control and permission structures [[117](https://docs.lyzr.ai/agent-lab/responsible-safe-ai/Responsible)].
*   **Negotiation State Engine:** Building a state management system to track the progress of the negotiation, store offers, counter-offers, and other relevant context .
*   **Contract Compiler/Validator:** Developing a component that can parse the agreed-upon terms and generate a formal contract, or validate that the outcome adheres to predefined constraints .
*   **Mock Integration Layer:** Using public APIs or direct function calls to simulate interactions with an external system, demonstrating how the agents could integrate into a larger workflow .

This approach allows the developer to construct a complete, end-to-end simulation that mirrors the architecture of a real-world production system. The interfaces, agent graph, and policy engine would be designed with production in mind, even though the underlying data sources are synthetic . The resulting system would be a compelling demonstration of a multi-agent collaboration pattern, where a central orchestrator decomposes a high-level objective (e.g., "negotiate a purchase order") into sub-tasks assigned to specialized agents [[16](https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/the-5-agentic-ai-design-patterns-ctos-should-evaluate-before-choosing-an-architecture)]. This architectural pattern is ideal for enterprise-grade automation as it improves consistency and creates a clear audit trail [[16](https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/the-5-agentic-ai-design-patterns-ctos-should-evaluate-before-choosing-an-architecture)].

In terms of evaluation against the specified criteria, PS02 scores strongly on the primary dimensions of governance and production readiness. The problem is fundamentally about governance, making it an excellent fit for the 30% weight assigned to "Governed, production-ready execution." A successful submission would showcase a system where each agent's actions are strictly bounded by a deterministic policy engine, a hallmark of responsible AI [[124](https://www.maviklabs.com/blog/agent-safety-policy-engine-2026/)]. The inclusion of Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) checkpoints for final approvals is a standard and necessary practice for such workflows, adding a critical layer of accountability for decisions with financial or legal consequences [[16](https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/the-5-agentic-ai-design-patterns-ctos-should-evaluate-before-choosing-an-architecture)]. This directly aligns with judging priorities that emphasize Lyzr ecosystem depth and robust governance frameworks . The system would also leverage Lyzr's "Responsible AI" module, incorporating features like prompt injection protection and PII redaction to ensure the agents operate securely and compliantly from the ground up [[117](https://docs.lyzr.ai/agent-lab/responsible-safe-ai/Responsible)].

The score for "Safety, deterministic controls & failure handling" (20% weight) is medium-high. In this context, safety primarily manifests as adherence to business rules and contractual parameters. The agent must be prevented from violating its budget, timeline, or other negotiated constraints. This can be effectively achieved through deterministic pre-action authorization checks, where every proposed move is vetted against the policy before being executed [[121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20953), [125](https://www.braiviq.com/playbook/pre-action-authorization-tool-call-governance-ai-agents-code-deterministic-policy-permission-2026)]. While the risk of a catastrophic failure is lower compared to action-oriented problems like PS03, a poorly designed negotiation agent could still lead to significant business losses by accepting suboptimal deals or making unauthorized commitments. Therefore, robust deterministic controls are essential for a successful entry.

Regarding "Beyond-the-wrapper" innovation (15% weight), the potential is medium. The core innovation is not simply having two LLMs negotiate; that is a well-understood pattern. The true differentiation lies in the novel mechanisms for ensuring safe and effective convergence. For example, the developer could implement a unique algorithm for policy alignment, a sophisticated HITL integration that allows for seamless human intervention without breaking the agent's state, or a reflection mechanism where the agents evaluate their own reasoning before proposing a deal [[16](https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/the-5-agentic-ai-design-patterns-ctos-should-evaluate-before-choosing-an-architecture)]. The Pactum model suggests that innovation is often found in the meticulous application of agentic principles to solve specific, high-stakes business problems, rather than reinventing the underlying technology [[15](https://pactum.com/blog/how-enterprise-procurement-leaders-use-ai)]. By focusing on these subtle but critical enhancements, a solo developer can create a truly differentiated entry.

Finally, the problem offers strong feasibility for objective benchmarking. Success can be measured quantitatively against a predefined set of negotiation scenarios. Key performance indicators could include the agreement rate (the percentage of negotiations that result in a successful contract), the time-to-negotiation-completion, and the degree of adherence to policy constraints (e.g., did the agent stay within the budget?). These metrics provide a clear, objective way to demonstrate the effectiveness and reliability of the developed system. The entire project, from synthetic data generation to the final validation, is achievable within the scope of a hackathon, allowing the developer to present a polished and impressive demonstration of governed, autonomous capability.

## Problem Statement 03: Action Safety

Problem Statement 03, centered on "action safety," represents the most technically challenging and strategically impactful option for a solo developer. The core challenge is to prevent autonomous agents from performing dangerous or unauthorized actions in a production-like environment, with a specific focus on preventing hazardous infrastructure mutations . This problem speaks directly to one of the most significant barriers to enterprise AI adoption: trust and safety [[75](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/enterprise-ai-moves-from-pilot-to-production-in-2026-but-gaps-in-governance-and-talent-persist)]. The attached analysis correctly identifies this as the area with the highest potential for differentiation, highlighting the need for Safe AI and Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) controls . Unlike the other problems, which involve a degree of abstraction, PS03 requires the developer to confront the concrete reality of agent execution. The feasibility analysis confirms that this is achievable within a hackathon setting by assuming the absence of a live production environment and instead building a comprehensive simulation . This approach allows the developer to construct a production-ready architecture from the ground up, using a sandboxed execution environment to safely test autonomous actions.

The competitive landscape for AIOps is crowded, with established vendors like Dynatrace, LogicMonitor, BigPanda, and AppDynamics offering sophisticated platforms for monitoring and incident response [[20](https://openobserve.ai/blog/top-10-aiops-platforms/), [21](https://www.cio.com/article/188989/top-aiops-platforms.html)]. These platforms excel at correlating alerts and identifying root causes, but their primary focus is often on detection and human-assisted remediation rather than on preventing autonomous actions in the first place. This gap presents a clear opportunity for a solution focused squarely on the "action safety" dimension. The emerging consensus in the industry is that prompt-based safety is insufficient; true safety requires enforcing authorization and least privilege at the point of tool execution [[3](https://www.osohq.com/learn/why-prompt-based-safety-is-not-enough)]. This has led to the rise of dedicated security platforms like Fencio, which enforces behavioral boundaries deterministically at runtime, and open-source toolkits like Microsoft's Agent Governance Toolkit, which adds a policy enforcement layer between the agent and its actions [[94](https://fencio.dev/), [96](https://awesomeagents.ai/news/microsoft-agent-governance-toolkit/)]. A hackathon entry for PS03 can position itself as a prototype of this emerging class of safety-critical infrastructure.

The viability of building a full-fledged simulation for PS03 is high, thanks to the explicit guidance in the feasibility rules . A solo developer can realistically construct the following components:
*   **Sandboxed Execution Environment:** This is the cornerstone of the project. Instead of connecting to a live Kubernetes cluster, the developer can build a mock executor or use a tool like Minikube to create a sandboxed environment where actions can be simulated without causing real-world damage . This directly addresses the requirement for a controlled testing ground.
*   **Mock Telemetry Generator:** To provide the agent with realistic input, a synthetic generator can be created to produce Prometheus-style alerts, Kubernetes events, and logs, simulating the kind of data an AIOps agent would analyze .
*   **Runbook Library:** A collection of deterministic procedures for responding to specific types of alerts must be created. These runbooks represent the "safe" actions the agent is authorized to take, forming the basis of its operational playbook .
*   **Policy Engine and HITL Integration:** A critical component is the policy engine that defines what an agent can and cannot access and do [[109](https://softwareanalyst.substack.com/p/runtime-security-for-ai-agents-an)]. This engine, combined with a mandatory HITL gate, ensures that no action is taken without proper authorization, a non-negotiable feature for any high-risk system [[16](https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/the-5-agentic-ai-design-patterns-ctos-should-evaluate-before-choosing-an-architecture)].

This simulation is not a mere facade; it is the production architecture. The developer is forced to build the very layers of control that are missing in many current agentic systems. The resulting system embodies a modern, secure agentic architecture, which decouples reasoning, memory, tool execution, and governance into distinct, scalable layers [[119](https://princetonits.com/autonomous-ai-agents-enterprise-automation/)]. This approach is superior to simply mocking the inputs and outputs because it forces the developer to engineer the entire chain of command, from decision to action, within a controlled environment. This directly demonstrates an understanding of the challenges faced by enterprises trying to deploy agents safely [[76](https://agatsoftware.com/ai-agent-security-enterprise-2026/)].

When evaluated against the specified criteria, PS03 scores exceptionally well, particularly on the most heavily weighted dimensions. Its score for "Governed, production-ready execution" (30% weight) is "Very High." This problem is the epitome of a production-ready capability. It compels the developer to build the most critical components of a reliable system: a deterministic control plane, a sandboxed execution environment, and a robust HITL workflow. The architecture required—a harness that translates an agent's reasoning into dependable, deterministic action—is a hallmark of enterprise-grade systems [[113](https://medium.com/@adnanmasood/agent-harness-engineering-the-rise-of-the-ai-control-plane-938ead884b1d)]. The project would serve as a powerful proof-of-concept for Lyzr's positioning as an Agentic OS with a unified deployment and governance layer [[7](https://www.lyzr.ai/blog/agentic-os-for-marketing/), [130](https://ittech-pulse.com/news/lyzr-launches-control-plane-for-enterprise-ai-agent-deployment-and-governance/)].

The score for "Safety, deterministic controls & failure handling" (20% weight) is "Highest." This is the central theme of the problem statement. A successful entry can credibly demonstrate a multi-layered safety strategy that reflects the state of the art. This includes: 1) **Sandboxing**, which contains the blast radius of any unauthorized or erroneous actions [[121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20953)]; 2) a **policy engine and HITL gate**, which provides a mandatory checkpoint for human approval before any high-risk action is taken [[16](https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/the-5-agentic-ai-design-patterns-ctos-should-evaluate-before-choosing-an-architecture)]; and 3) **deterministic pre-action authorization**, where a guardrail runs before the tool call to verify it is permitted by the policy, making it impossible for prompt injection to bypass the check [[92](https://github.com/aporthq/aport-agent-guardrails)]. This combination of containment, verification, and approval provides a comprehensive defense-in-depth strategy for agentic safety, making for a highly impressive and defensible demonstration.

The potential for "Beyond-the-wrapper" innovation (15% weight) is "High." Here, the innovation is not just differentiating but is central to the project's success. The "wrapper" is the LLM; the innovation is the entire surrounding layer of safety and control. A developer can build upon proven patterns like the Microsoft Agent Governance Toolkit [[96](https://awesomeagents.ai/news/microsoft-agent-governance-toolkit/)] and add novel features to stand out. For instance, they could implement a dynamic runbook generation system that learns from past incidents, or develop a sophisticated behavioral monitoring framework to detect anomalous agent behavior in real-time [[87](https://ieeeusa.org/assets/public-policy/policy-log/2026/IEEE-USA-NIST-RFI-Agentic-AI-030926.pdf)]. Another avenue for innovation is providing verifiable evidence of a safe action, perhaps through a cryptographic proof-of-execution mechanism that cryptographically verifies that the declared guardrails were executed for an agent response [[158](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408572347_Proof_of_Execution_Runtime_Verification_for_Governed_AI_Agent_Actions)]. This focus on the outer safety layer makes for a compelling narrative and a technically advanced project.

Finally, the feasibility of objective benchmarking is "Very Strong." Performance can be rigorously measured against a known dataset of simulated incidents. Metrics could include Mean Time to Safe Resolution (MTTSR), the accuracy of the agent's root cause identification, and the ratio of autonomous resolutions to human-approved interventions. The existence of benchmarks for evaluating AI agents' safety and effectiveness, such as those from Carnegie Mellon, reinforces the validity of this approach [[162](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-agent-benchmarks)]. A developer could even introduce adversarial inputs to test the robustness of their safety mechanisms, providing a clear and quantitative measure of their system's resilience.

## Problem Statement 04: Traceability & Lineage

Problem Statement 04, "Traceability & Lineage," addresses the critical need for auditable, transparent, and explainable AI systems. The core challenge is to build an infrastructure that can create a verifiable, tamper-proof trail connecting an AI agent's final action back to its initial intent, the data it processed, and the policies it followed . This problem is deeply intertwined with governance and regulatory compliance, as modern regulations increasingly mandate such transparency. The attached analysis correctly reframes the problem away from simple carbon calculations and toward the more fundamental task of source-to-result lineage, emphasizing the importance of a deterministic calculation engine combined with a robust classification and auditing system . Like the others, it assumes the absence of enterprise systems like ERPs or commercial ESG data pipelines, requiring the developer to build everything from synthetic data upwards .

The competitive landscape for this problem is dominated by mature ESG platforms such as Watershed, Persefoni, and Sweep, as well as data lineage specialists . These companies have invested heavily in integrating with complex, real-world enterprise data sources, making it impractical to replicate their full functionality in a hackathon. However, the underlying concepts of traceability and lineage are far more general and are becoming a major theme across the entire AI industry, driven largely by regulatory pressures. The EU AI Act, for example, imposes strict record-keeping obligations on high-risk AI systems, requiring them to automatically log events to enable traceability and post-market monitoring [[120](https://www.dataiku.com/blog/eu-ai-act-high-risk-requirements), [176](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-2026-compliance), [186](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/)]. Similarly, open standards like OpenLineage have emerged to provide a common language for capturing metadata about data pipelines, with support for detailed column-level lineage [[11](https://atlan.com/data-lineage-tools/), [13](https://www.ovaledge.com/blog/ai-powered-open-source-data-lineage-tools), [152](https://github.com/OpenLineage/OpenLineage)]. This indicates that while the specific domain may be competitive, the core technical challenge of building a verifiable audit trail is both timely and broadly applicable.

The feasibility for a solo developer is high. The project is entirely self-contained and does not depend on third-party services. The developer can create all necessary components:
*   **Synthetic Datasets:** Generating synthetic invoices, travel/freight data, and other relevant information is a manageable task that forms the raw material for the agent's work .
*   **Classification and Calculation Engine:** An AI agent can be built to classify the synthetic data (e.g., categorizing expenses or calculating a carbon footprint) using a deterministic formula. This combines AI-driven classification with rule-based computation, as suggested by the source analysis .
*   **Lineage Store:** The core of the project is the creation of a purpose-built lineage store. This database would track the provenance of every piece of data, the transformations applied by the agent, and the final output, creating a complete source-to-result map.
*   **Report Generator:** A component to visualize the results and, crucially, the generated lineage, providing a clear and understandable audit trail.

This problem uniquely blurs the line between a hackathon simulation and a production architecture. The synthetic data sources become the "enterprise systems," and the developer builds the entire lineage tracking infrastructure from the ground up. This forces a deep and practical understanding of what constitutes a complete and defensible audit trail. The architecture should incorporate modern data governance concepts, such as capturing fine-grained, column-level lineage to understand exactly which input columns produced which output columns [[153](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/facets/dataset-facets/column_lineage_facet/)], and storing immutable records of decisions to allow for accurate historical reconstruction [[62](https://medium.com/@hydra_db/whats-the-best-way-to-trace-ai-agent-decisions-and-ensure-auditability-in-2026-a9ca1d43f748)]. The resulting system would be a powerful demonstration of a key pillar of AI governance.

In terms of evaluation, PS04 scores very highly on "Governed, production-ready execution" (30% weight). The ability to generate a tamper-proof audit trail is a cornerstone of responsible AI and a core tenet of modern governance frameworks [[22](https://www.sirion.ai/library/contract-insights/ai-governance-frameworks-clm/), [61](https://www.lyzr.ai/ai-agents/ai-agents-for-ai-audit-trails/)]. A successful submission would demonstrate a system that generates verifiable evidence for every action, satisfying requirements from international standards like ISO/IEC 42001 and legal mandates like the EU AI Act [[57](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-compliance-frameworks-compared-eu-act-nist-rmf-fvsxc), [58](https://www.zengrc.com/blog/navigating-the-future-of-ai-governance-a-guide-to-nist-ai-rmf-iso-iec-42001-and-the-eu-ai-act/)]. The project would showcase Lyzr's capacity for creating immutable logs and granular access controls, key features for ensuring compliance [[61](https://www.lyzr.ai/ai-agents/ai-agents-for-ai-audit-trails/), [117](https://docs.lyzr.ai/agent-lab/responsible-safe-ai/Responsible)]. This directly addresses the judging emphasis on Lyzr ecosystem depth and governance.

The score for "Safety, deterministic controls & failure handling" (20% weight) is "Medium." The primary benefit of traceability is indirect. It enhances safety by promoting transparency and accountability, which enables thorough post-hoc investigation and learning. If an agent makes a mistake, the lineage allows developers and operators to understand *why* it happened and take steps to prevent recurrence. This is a form of safety, but it is largely reactive rather than proactive. Unlike PS03, which provides mechanisms to prevent a failure from occurring, PS04 provides the tools to analyze it afterward. The safety mechanism is preventative through improved understanding, not through immediate intervention. However, in domains like finance or healthcare, this level of auditability is a critical safety requirement for regulatory compliance [[240](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/audit-trail-requirements-ai-gxp-compliance)].

The potential for "Beyond-the-wrapper" innovation (15% weight) is "Medium." While the concept of lineage is well-established, the novelty can come from the specific implementation details. For example, the developer could implement a bitemporal graph database to store decision traces, which allows for accurate "as-of" context replay queries by separating system time from valid time [[62](https://medium.com/@hydra_db/whats-the-best-way-to-trace-ai-agent-decisions-and-ensure-auditability-in-2026-a9ca1d43f748)]. This is a sophisticated approach that goes beyond simple logging. Alternatively, the innovation could lie in the user experience, such as building an intuitive visualizer that makes complex data lineage easy to understand, similar to tools like Atlan or DataHub that offer visual column-level lineage [[11](https://atlan.com/data-lineage-tools/), [154](https://pdpspectra.com/blog/openlineage-column-level-lineage/)]. The innovation is in the *infrastructure* for traceability and the methods used to make it accessible and useful.

Objective benchmarking for PS04 is "Strong." The completeness and accuracy of the generated lineage can be measured quantitatively. The developer can create a series of test cases with known inputs and expected lineage paths. By introducing errors or ambiguities into the synthetic data, they can test whether the lineage store accurately captures the point of failure. Metrics could include lineage coverage (what percentage of data provenance was tracked) and lineage accuracy (how precisely the relationships between inputs and outputs were captured). This provides a solid, evidence-based way to validate the system's performance and reliability.

Showing an agent analyze a simulated cloud alert, request human approval, and then execute a corrective action in a contained sandbox is a dramatically effective way to prove that an autonomous agent can be trusted with real-world responsibilities.
